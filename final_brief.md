# A student café can be a useful campus service—if tested before it is scaled

**Decision requested:** approve a time-limited, measured café pilot, not an unconditional full-scale commitment. A university café study surveying 201 students found that food quality, ambience, value for money, menu variety and service quality were associated with satisfaction and dining frequency; its convenience sample is directional evidence, not a forecast for this campus [1]. An Indian café price comparison and a campus-wide spending survey are still needed before final pricing.

**Offer and pricing hypothesis:** test a small menu—chai ₹25, filter coffee ₹45, cappuccino ₹90 and a snack ₹55. These are proposed planning prices, not verified market averages or supplier quotes. Check nearby café menus, campus affordability and ingredient costs before launch. Keep a clearly marked plant-based choice; a four-country study including India found willingness-to-consume segments differ across plant-food categories, so do not assume universal demand [2]. Track unsold food daily; UNEP recommends measuring food-service food waste as a basis for reduction [3].

**Illustrative monthly break-even (all figures are assumptions):** fixed costs = rent/facilities ₹60,000 + 3 staff × ₹18,000 = ₹54,000 + utilities ₹15,000 + equipment/maintenance ₹12,000 + cleaning/admin/miscellaneous ₹9,000 = **₹1,50,000**. At average spend ₹110 per transaction and variable cost 35% of sales, contribution = ₹110 × (1 − 0.35) = **₹71.50 per transaction**. Monthly break-even = ₹1,50,000 ÷ ₹71.50 = 2,097.9, rounded up to **2,098 transactions**. Over 26 open days: 2,098 ÷ 26 = 80.7, rounded up to **81 transactions/day**—about 1.0% of 8,000 enrolled students as daily transactions, not unique customers. At 81/day, estimated monthly revenue is ₹2,31,660 and contribution ₹1,50,579, only ₹579 above assumed fixed costs. This excludes taxes, financing, startup costs and demand variation; it is not a profit forecast.

**Risk and next step:** demand, local rent/wages, food inflation, staffing, food safety and waste could move the result materially. Before releasing a permanent budget, survey students, obtain local quotes, validate menu prices, and run a short pilot measuring daily transactions, average bill, contribution margin and food waste. Continue only if observed results support a revised, fully costed case.

4. [Tata Starbucks India menu](https://www.starbucks.in/menu) — a competitor-check starting point; its public page did not expose item-level prices during this brief's preparation. Proposed prices above are explicitly assumptions, not claimed observations.

**Arithmetic audit:** 60,000 + (3 × 18,000) + 15,000 + 12,000 + 9,000 = 150,000; 110 × 0.65 = 71.50; 150,000 ÷ 71.50 = 2,097.9; ceil(2,097.9 ÷ 26) = 81 transactions/day; 81 × 26 × 110 × 0.65 − 150,000 = ₹579.
