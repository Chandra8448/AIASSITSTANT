## Study guide

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert
light energy into chemical energy stored in organic molecules. In plants, it
primarily takes place in chloroplasts. Chlorophyll and other pigments absorb
light; light-dependent reactions use that energy to produce ATP and NADPH.
The Calvin cycle then uses ATP and NADPH to incorporate carbon dioxide into
sugars. The simplified overall equation is
`6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2`; it summarizes a complex set
of reactions rather than describing a single step.

### Key terms

- **Chloroplast:** cell structure where photosynthesis occurs in plants and algae.
- **ATP and NADPH:** molecules that transfer usable energy and reducing power
  from light-dependent reactions to the Calvin cycle.
- **Calvin cycle:** reactions that use carbon dioxide, ATP, and NADPH to build
  carbohydrate molecules.

### Quick summary

Light energy powers reactions that make energy-carrying molecules; those
molecules help convert carbon dioxide into sugars. Oxygen is released as a
by-product of the water-splitting light reactions.

## Quiz

1. **Multiple choice:** What is the main role of chlorophyll?
   A. Absorb light energy. B. Store genetic information. C. Break down glucose.
2. **Short answer:** What do ATP and NADPH provide to the Calvin cycle?
3. **Multiple choice:** Which molecule is incorporated into sugars during the
   Calvin cycle? A. Carbon dioxide. B. Nitrogen gas. C. Sodium chloride.
4. **Short answer:** Why is the overall photosynthesis equation described as
   simplified?
5. **Multiple choice:** Which is released as a by-product of the light
   reactions? A. Oxygen. B. Methane. C. Nitrogen.

## Answer key

1. **A.** Pigments including chlorophyll absorb light that drives
   photosynthesis.
2. They provide energy and reducing power used in carbon fixation and sugar
   production.
3. **A.** The Calvin cycle incorporates carbon from carbon dioxide into
   organic molecules.
4. It summarizes many reactions and intermediates as a net equation; it is
   not a single reaction or the full mechanism.
5. **A.** Oxygen is released when water is split during the light-dependent
   reactions.
